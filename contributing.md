# Contribution Guidelines

Thanks for wanting to add something. This list is meant to stay short enough to read in one sitting, so the bar is "would someone working with Aerospike be glad you told them about this".

## Adding to the list

- **One pull request per suggestion.** It makes each one easy to discuss on its own merits.
- **Use the existing format:** `- [Name](link) - Description.` The description starts with a capital letter, ends with a period, and does not repeat the name.
- **Keep the description to one line.** Say what the thing is and what it is for, not why it is great.
- **Put the entry in the right section, in alphabetical order** where the section is alphabetical.
- **Link to the project, not to its documentation page** — unless the documentation *is* the resource.
- Explain in the pull request why it belongs here. A sentence is enough.

## What gets accepted

- Anything that works with Aerospike and is usable today: clients, tools, connectors, operational tooling, learning material.
- Both official and community projects. Neither is preferred over the other.
- Commercial software, as long as the entry says so plainly and there is something a reader can actually use or evaluate.

## What does not

- **Abandoned projects.** If nothing has moved in years and it no longer works against a current server, it sends people down a dead end.
- **Anything that needs a login or a sales conversation before you can read it.** Gated white papers and webinar sign-ups are not resources.
- **Aerospike rocket nozzles.** The name collides with an entirely unrelated field of aerospace engineering, and those projects have their own community.
- Single blog posts, conference talks or slide decks. Link the blog, not the post.
- Your own project presented as something other than your own project. Saying "I wrote this" costs nothing and is not held against you.

## Fixing something

Broken links, projects that have moved, entries that have gone stale — open a pull request or an issue. Removals are as welcome as additions; a list that only ever grows stops being curated.
